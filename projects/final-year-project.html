Final Year Project

This project built an experimental rig that fires a high-speed jet of air along a heated metal plate, then used a color-based optical technique to measure how the air's density changes near the hot surface, benchmarked against CFD simulations.

Objective

  • Design and characterize a compressible jet facility — a converging nozzle paired with a temperature-controlled flat plate.
  • Calibrate a Rainbow Schlieren Deflectometry (RSD) system to extract quantitative density-gradient, density, and temperature fields for the wall jet.
  • Run 2D CFD simulations of the same flow and compare them with the experimental data.
  • Develop and validate pyrsd, an open-source Python library for RSD processing.

Approach

  • 3D-printed a converging nozzle (12 mm × 5 mm exit) fed by a compressor, producing a high-subsonic jet (up to M ≈ 0.97) over a Peltier-heated, PID-controlled metal plate held at 40°C and 60°C.
  • Used a Z-type double-mirror schlieren system with custom rainbow color filters, fabricated on E6 slide film after early digitally-printed versions showed poor transmissivity.
  • Built pyrsd, a Python library covering hue calibration, per-frame displacement extraction, ensemble averaging, and density reconstruction via 1D integration and 2D Poisson inversion.
  • Validated the pipeline against natural convection from the heated plate before applying it to the wall jet, then compared results with 2D RANS CFD (ANSYS Fluent, k-ω SST).

Key Results

  • RSD recovered the plate's known surface temperature from natural-convection images to within ~0.3–1% of the thermocouple setpoint, validating the pipeline.
  • Peak density gradient scaled with wall temperature: ≈0.016 kg/m³/mm at 40°C versus ≈0.026 kg/m³/mm at 60°C in the natural-convection case.
  • Schlieren imagery confirmed nozzle choking above ~1.9 bar gauge, matching the predicted critical pressure ratio, with oblique shock structures visible beyond that point.
  • CFD (k-ω SST) over-predicted near-wall density gradients relative to RSD by roughly an order of magnitude near the nozzle exit, narrowing further downstream.
  • pyrsd was released as an open-source library — the report notes no equivalent open, documented RSD processing tool existed previously.
